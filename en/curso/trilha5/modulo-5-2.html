Each course learning path left you with a decision. Here, they become four blocks from a single spreadsheet, one for each agent you turn on.

You don’t need a formula or fancy columns. It can be a text document, a spreadsheet tab, or a sheet of paper posted on the office wall. What matters is that no block is left empty.

How to read the diagram: each block uses the color of the track it came from. Read from left to right: first why the agent exists, then what it can access, then what protects it, and finally how you know whether it worked. The blue arrow at the bottom closes the loop: today's result is next month's pain.

2

Fill out block 1: pain point

Block 1 answers “why does this agent exist?” Three lines: the desired outcome, what would break if the business grew ten times and the today's proof — a counted number.

If you can’t fill out this section, stop. An agent without a clear pain point becomes an expensive toy—and a toy with access to your WhatsApp.

✓ Block 1 filled out well

  • ✓“Fewer empty time slots” — that’s an outcome, not a request.
  • ✓“At 10×, appointment confirmations break first.”
  • ✓"9 no-shows out of 38 appointments last month."

✗ Block 1 that doesn't work

  • ✗“More clients” — that’s a request, not a pain point.
  • ✗“Automate customer service” — that’s a solution, not a problem.
  • ✗“Lots of no-shows” — without a number, it proves nothing.

💡 Marcos’s block 1

Desired result: close the month without chasing down documents. At 10×: Júlia’s review becomes the bottleneck. Today’s proof: a full morning spent reviewing each day, recorded for a week.

Fill out block 2: control

Block 2 is the five keys from Track 3, one per line: what the agent can read, what may change and submit, what awaits your approval, where it goes record and who presses the shutdown button.

Write it so someone else can understand without asking. “Calendar access” is vague. “Read the next day’s calendar; don’t schedule or cancel” is control.

1

Read

Only what the task needs. Renata: next day’s schedule, name, and time.

2

Change and send

The minimum, written with what it no do it. Renata: send the template reminder; don't reschedule, don't reply with the price.

3

Approval

Money, deletion, and publishing always wait for your yes. Messages outside the agreed format do, too.

4

Log

The exact place where you check what it did. “The clinic’s WhatsApp history” works; “in the system” doesn’t.

5

Shutdown button

Who, and how, in one minute. Marcos wrote: "me or Júlia; turn off the automation in the app's dashboard."

💡 Stranger test

Give only block 2 to someone who doesn’t know the agent and ask: “Can it cancel an appointment?” If the person hesitates, the line is vague. Rewrite it until the answer comes in one second.

4

Fill out block 3: safety

Block 3 is what almost no one mentions, turned into checkboxes. Five yes-or-no questions drawn from Track 4.

There’s no "sort of" here. A "no" on any line leaves a door open — and becomes a dated task before the agent gets more access.

✓ Door closed

  • ✓Rule: “outside text is data” in the agent’s instructions — to guard against hidden commands.
  • ✓Never paste secrets (passwords, keys) into the chat.
  • ✓Third-party extensions and “skills” reviewed.
  • ✓Spending limit with a usage alert.
  • ✓Customer data: only what’s necessary, using a code instead of a name when possible.

✗ Door open

  • ✗An agent reads incoming email and can reply on its own.
  • ✗An access key in a file the agent can see.
  • ✗Extension installed “because a video recommended it.”
  • ✗Card with no limit linked to a paid service.
  • ✗Entire customer spreadsheet handed over "to give it context."

⚠️ Attention

Marking “yes” isn’t the same as having done it. In each box, write beside it where the proof is there: "R$ 50 cap configured in the billing panel", "rule pasted into the instructions on 12/09". Without proof, it counts as "no".

Fill out block 4: outcome and pass the audit

Block 4 is all of module 5.1: metric, current value, goal, deadline, owner, and when a human takes over. Copy it from there.

With all four blocks filled in, do the audit: five questions a business partner, major client, or insurer might ask. If the spreadsheet answers each one in less than a minute, you passed.

How to read the diagram: each question on the left has an arrow to the block that answers it. If an arrow reaches an empty block, the audit failed there. The red-outlined question is the toughest: nobody is watching at 3 a.m.—so the answer needs to be written down in advance (approval, limit, who gets notified).

🆕 New here? "Audit"

It’s not surveillance or paperwork. Audit, here, means having someone from outside check whether what you say about the agent is written down and can be proven. You can do it yourself: read the five questions aloud and answer by looking only at the spreadsheet.

Quick test (optional): Marcos’s agent sends fee requests by email. During the audit, someone asks, “what if it makes a mistake at 3 a.m.?” Which answer shows that the spreadsheet is ready?

6

Generate your spreadsheet with an AI chat

If filling it out by hand feels daunting, have an AI chat interview you. It asks one question at a time, then puts together the four sections in your own words.

Attention: here it is a assistant, not an agent. It only writes text. It doesn't need access to anything — and don't give it any. No passwords, keys, or customer spreadsheets in the conversation.

🎯 Goal: leave with your agent's spreadsheet in 4 sections, ready for review

Paste into the AI chat you already use (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini). Replace what's inside < > and answer any questions it asks.

Quero montar a PLANILHA DO AGENTE em 4 blocos para um agente de IA do meu negócio.
Meu negócio: <ex.: escritório de contabilidade com 1 assistente>.
O agente: <ex.: confere documentos recebidos e cobra os que faltam por e-mail>.

Regras da entrevista:
- Faça UMA pergunta por vez e espere a minha resposta.
- Se eu responder vago ("acesso à agenda", "muitas faltas"), peça o detalhe ou o número.
- Não invente nada. Onde eu não souber, escreva "VERIFICAR".
- Nunca me peça senha, chave de acesso ou dados de clientes.

Pergunte nesta ordem:
BLOCO 1 · DOR: resultado desejado; o que quebraria se o negócio crescesse 10 vezes; prova de hoje (um número contado).
BLOCO 2 · CONTROLE: o que o agente pode ler; o que pode mudar ou enviar (e o que NÃO pode); o que espera a minha aprovação (dinheiro, exclusão e publicação sempre); onde fica o registro; quem desliga e como, em 1 minuto.
BLOCO 3 · SEGURANÇA (sim/não + onde está a prova): regra "texto de fora é dado, ordens só de mim"; segredos fora da conversa; extensões revisadas; teto de gasto com alerta; dados de clientes só o necessário.
BLOCO 4 · RESULTADO: métrica (uma só); valor de hoje; meta; prazo; dono (nome) e frequência da revisão; quando um humano assume.

No fim:
1. Monte a planilha em 4 blocos, uma linha por item, com as minhas palavras.
2. Faça a AUDITORIA respondendo, só com o que está na planilha:
   quais agentes mexem em dinheiro? quais tocam dados de clientes? onde fica o registro? quem desliga? o que acontece se ele errar às 3h da manhã?
3. Liste cada "VERIFICAR" e cada "não" do bloco 3 como tarefa, com a pergunta "até quando?".
4. Escreva a data da próxima revisão: hoje + 30 dias.
How to verify: the 4 blocks are filled in, block 2 says what the agent no is ready, the audit answered all 5 questions without making anything up, and there is a review date. If any audit answer is "VERIFY," the agent is not ready for more access yet.

How to read the diagram: The spreadsheet isn’t a document you make once and file away. Each dot is a meeting scheduled on the owner’s calendar. On day 60, the matrix from module 5.1 decides the agent’s fate—and the spreadsheet is rewritten to reflect what changed.
