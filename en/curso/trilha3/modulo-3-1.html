🔑 The five keys

Read, change, approve, log, and shut off. Five decisions that fit on one page and define the agent’s scope before it exists.

1

Treat the agent like a new employee

No one hires someone on Monday and hands them the bank password that same day. They start with the basics and get access as they show they can handle it.

It's the same with an agent. Access isn’t all or nothing: you choose each item, and can grant more later.

Renata was going to connect the reminder agent to WhatsApp, the schedule, the patient spreadsheet, and the card reader. She stopped first and made a new list.

How to read the diagram: count the lines that leave each agent. There are four on the left, and the last ends in red, at the money. There are two on the right, and each says what it can do ("read only," "send"). The dashed boxes are tools that exist but that the agent can’t reach.

🆕 New here? “Least access possible”

It's the rule of giving the agent only the access the task requires — not one more tool. If the reminder works without the spreadsheet, leave the spreadsheet out. If you’re unsure about granting access, the answer is “no, for now.”

✓ New employee welcomed properly

  • ✓Start with just one task.
  • ✓It gets the keys to the rooms it will use.
  • ✓Get more access after proving your value.

✗ Agent connected to everything on day one

  • ✗Three tasks mixed into a single request.
  • ✗Access to all accounts “just in case it’s needed.”
  • ✗No one can say what it can or can't do.

2

Decide what it can read

The first key is the weakest—and the one most people hand over without thinking. Read seems harmless: it only looks, it doesn't change anything.

But what it reads may contain client data, and may include outside text giving instructions (that’s trail 4). So the question is: to do this task, what does it need to see — and only that?

TaskNeed to readYou don’t need to read
Appointment reminder (Renata)next day’s calendar: name, time, phone numbermedical record, payment history, photos
Review documents (Marcos)files that arrive on the office’s WhatsApppersonal email, statements from all clients
Answer questions about hours (store)business hours, addresscustomer list, inventory, invoices

What to look for in the table: The middle column is always short. When the "needs to read" list gets long, be suspicious: you’ve probably combined two tasks in one agent.

💡 Practical tip

Prefer to give a folder to give the entire disk; a tab from the spreadsheet to giving them the whole spreadsheet; one calendar separate from your personal calendar. Almost every app lets you filter it this way — look for "share only this folder" or "choose calendar".

3

Decide what it can change and send

The second key is where the agent stops looking and starts changing things. Change is to change something of yours: a spreadsheet row, a calendar time. Send is to send something out: a message, an email, a file.

They’re not the same level. You can undo a change inside the house. You can’t take back a message that reached a client.

How to read the diagram: each level gives the agent more power and increases the damage a mistake can cause. The purple levels (reading and drafting) cause almost no harm. The blue ones already touch real things. The red one can't be undone. For each task, ask: How many levels up does it need to go?

✓ Well-written changes and sends

  • ✓“You may write only in the Conferência tab.”
  • ✓“You may send reminders only, using the approved template.”
  • ✓“You may send messages only to people with an appointment tomorrow.”

✗ Changes and sends left open

  • ✗“You may organize the spreadsheet however you think best.”
  • ✗“You may reply to clients.”
  • ✗“You may send a message to the list.”

4

Decide what needs your approval

The third key stays with you. Approval is the moment when the agent stops, shows you what it’s about to do, and waits for a person to say “yes.”

You don’t need to approve everything—or the agent just becomes more work. But three things always go through a person, without exception: money, deletion, and publishing.

How to read the diagram: the shining gate is you. Nothing reaches the blue box (the real world) without passing through it. If the answer is “no,” the red arrow sends the work back to the agent—the mistake dies there, before it gets out. The three boxes below are the actions that always get through the gate.

Always with approvalExample at the clinicWhy
💳 Moneycharge a deposit, issue a refund, generate a payment slipmoney that’s gone rarely comes back on its own
🗑️ Deletiondelete a "duplicate" patient, clear the calendarwithout a copy, there's no way to recover
📢 PublishingInstagram post, promotion to the lista lot of people see it before you delete it

⚠️ Attention

“Approve everything with one click without reading” isn’t approval. If you catch yourself automatically clicking “yes,” the agent is asking for approval too often—or you’re at the wrong level. Reduce what goes through the gate, but read what does.

5

Ensure there’s a record and an off switch

The last two keys are for when something has already gone wrong. The record says what the agent did. The shutdown button make it stop.

Without a record, no one knows what to fix. Without a shutoff button—or with a button only one person knows how to find—the error keeps happening while you search.

🆕 New here? Log and shutoff switch

  • Log (or history, log) — the list of what the agent did, with date and time: "10:02 a.m. sent a reminder to Ana," "10:03 a.m. checked the calendar." Many apps already keep this; look for "activity" or "history" in the settings.
  • Shutdown button — the fastest way to stop the agent: pause the automation, disconnect WhatsApp, remove email access. It has to work in one minute, without needing you.

Marcos tested this before he needed it. See what happened during the week he went on vacation:

1

Before vacation

Marcos showed his assistant Júlia where the log is and how to disconnect email in the agent settings. She repeated the steps on her own.

2

Tuesday, 9:40 a.m.

A customer calls: they received two charges for a pending document, but the document had already been submitted.

3

9:41 a.m. — shutoff button

Júlia disconnects the agent’s email. It took less than a minute. No new messages go out.

4

9:50 a.m. — record

Using the history, she finds the 4 customers who received the duplicate charge and calls each one. Marcos only finds out when she gets back, with everything resolved.

💡 Minute test

Ask the person who will turn off the agent to do it now, with a clock in hand, without your help. If it takes more than a minute, or they need to call you, the off button doesn't exist yet—it only exists in your head.

Fill out the five-controls worksheet

Now put the five keys on one sheet. This is just planning: nothing changes in your app yet. It takes about ten minutes.

Use the agent you chose in module 1.2 and the process you defined in track 2. Skipped both? Think of any task you'd like to hand off to an agent.

🎯 Goal: leave with the AGENT SHEET filled out, with a name in "turn off"

Copy this to your phone's Notes or to a piece of paper. Replace what's between < >. If you'd like help, paste it into the AI chat you use and ask: "ask me one question at a time until this form is filled out."

FICHA DO AGENTE: <nome da tarefa, ex.: lembrete de consultas>
Dono (quem responde por ele): <nome>

1. LER: <o que ele pode ver — e só isso>
2. MUDAR E ENVIAR: <o que ele pode alterar ou mandar, e para quem>
3. APROVAÇÃO: <o que espera o meu sim antes de acontecer>
4. REGISTRO: <onde confiro o que ele fez, com dia e hora>
5. DESLIGAR: <quem desliga, e como, em até 1 minuto>
   Substituto na minha ausência: <nome>

Sempre com aprovação: dinheiro, exclusão, publicação.
Fora de alcance (de propósito): <ferramentas que ele NÃO recebe>
How to verify: all five lines have answers, none says "everything," and "shutdown" has a name that isn't just yours. Show the sheet to that person and run the one-minute test.

💡 Example: Renata’s form

  • Read: next day’s calendar (name, time, phone number).
  • Change and send: only reminders through the clinic's WhatsApp. It doesn't reschedule or cancel.
  • Approval: the reminder template; the agent only changes the name and time. Any other message waits for Renata.
  • Log: the clinic’s WhatsApp message history.
  • Turn off: Renata disconnects WhatsApp in the agent settings—or the receptionist does it when she’s unavailable.
  • Out of scope: patient spreadsheet and card reader.

Quick test (optional): a sales agent will issue payment slips to customers. Which control must not be missing?
