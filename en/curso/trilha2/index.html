💥 The 10× question

Problem before the tool: the question that reveals the bottleneck, the numbers that prove it, and the choice of a single process.

0 of 60%
What it is:

“If the business grew 10 times tomorrow, which process would break first?” The question exaggerates the workload so the weak point becomes visible.

Why learn:

An agent executes the plan quickly, whether it's right or wrong. Choosing the problem matters more than choosing the tool.

Key concepts:

Process, bottleneck, the 10× question, problem before the tool.

What it is:

A filter: if no one does that for a week, will the business lose a customer, money, or a deadline? If so, break it down. If not, it's just annoying.

Why learn:

The list of complaints mixes the two things together. Automating an annoyance wastes effort and opens a door without a payoff.

Key concepts:

Breaks, annoyance, this week's test, queue.

What it is:

During a typical week, track how much time each candidate takes, how much money is lost, and how often things go wrong.

Why learn:

Your guess usually points to what’s visible. The number points to what costs you—in Marcos’s case, 10 hours per week.

Key concepts:

Time, money, error, estimate labeled.

What it is:

Common selection mistakes: automating what’s fun or visible (posts, videos, little bots) instead of what quietly costs you.

Why learn:

Every agent is another door to control. And bringing in more customers for a process that leaks only makes the leak worse.

Key concepts:

Cost × visibility matrix, good and bad signs, data leak.

What it is:

A ready-to-use template to paste into an AI chat: it interviews you, one question at a time, until it finds the process that's breaking and the number that proves it.

Why learn:

It’s easy to dodge the question on your own. The chat keeps you focused—and you talk about the process, never the customers.

Key concepts:

Interview, one question at a time, don’t make up numbers.

What it is:

Four steps to decide: make a list, put numbers to it, cut what affects money, and write "I'll start with X because it costs Y."

Why learn:

One agent at a time is the only way to know whether it worked and stayed under control.

Key concepts:

Just one, a waitlist, a low-cost error, a decision statement.

🏥 A request isn't a problem

Renata’s clinic case: behind the request for “more clients” was a schedule that was losing R$ 1.080 per month. From pricing the pain to the first small project.

0 of 60%
What it is:

The request is the ready-made solution you’re given (“more clients”). The desire is what’s behind it (“more revenue”). The pain is the loss you can count.

Why learn:

Following the request to the letter can be costly and still not solve the problem. Asking “what for?” leads to the number.

Key concepts:

Request, desire, pain point, "what for?".

What it is:

Renata wanted more patients through Instagram. The schedule showed something else: patients who booked and didn’t show up.

Why learn:

More people joining a broken schedule only increases no-shows. Fix the leak before opening the faucet.

Key concepts:

Absences, paid hours without revenue, a leaky bucket.

What it is:

Absences × average value × weeks. At the clinic: 9 absences × R$ 120 = R$ 1,080 per month, almost R$ 13,000 per year.

Why learn:

Without a price, the pain is just an opinion. With a price, it becomes a decision—and later, the metric for track 5.

Key concepts:

How many times, how many fail, how much can be recovered, the cost of the pain.

What it is:

An agent that sends a reminder two days ahead, asks for confirmation, and records the response. Rescheduling and fitting people in go through Renata.

Why learn:

The first project needs to fail cheaply. In the worst case, one extra reminder goes out.

Key concepts:

Small project, timeline, approval, low-cost error.

What it is:

The same pattern in other businesses: the request points outward, while the pain is inside. Marcos asked for speed; the pain was overdue documents turning into fines.

Why learn:

Recognizing the pattern in other people’s businesses helps you see it in your own.

Key concepts:

Sounds like a request × sounds like a pain point, first project by profession.

What it is:

“Today we lose ___ because of ___ because ___; the first agent will ___.” A template to review with an AI chat.

Why learn:

It's the first field in the agent's profile and the starting point for the Track 3 controls.

Key concepts:

Pain statement, number, cause you can change, small action.
