MODULE 2.1

💥 The 10× question

Before choosing the tool, choose the problem. One simple question shows what breaks first in your business — and that’s where an agent can help most.

1

Ask the 10× question

An agent executes quickly. If the plan is wrong, it executes the mistake quickly, too. That's why the first decision isn't "which tool," but what problem.

One question can help you find the weak spot without a spreadsheet or consultant: "if my business grew 10 times overnight, which process would break first?"

No one actually needs to grow 10 times. The question just exaggerates the change so the weak point becomes clear. Whatever would break at 10× is already creaking today, just quietly.

🆕 New here? Two words from this track

  • Process — a task that repeats the same way every time: schedule an appointment, collect payment, check a document, respond to a quote request.
  • Bottleneck — the narrowest point in the process, where work piles up. Like the neck of a bottle: no matter how big it is, water flows at the pace of the spout.

How to read the diagram: the thick blue arrows show the volume coming in; the dashed gray arrows show what can get through. All the extra flow gets stuck at the lit-up box. Improving "service" or "billing" won't help while the lit-up box is clogged.

2

Separate what breaks from what’s merely annoying

When you ask the 10× question, a list of complaints appears. Not everything on it is a bottleneck. Some of it is just nuisance: tedious and repetitive, but it doesn’t block anything.

The test is to ask what happens if no one does that for a week. If the business loses a customer, money, or a deadline, breaks. If it only gets uglier or slower, it’s annoying.

💥 Breakdown

  • ✓A patient books and no one confirms: an empty time slot, a paid-for room.
  • ✓The client’s document arrives late: the guide expires, and a fine appears.
  • ✓No reply to a quote within 24h: the customer goes with someone else.
  • ✓Forgotten billing: money that never comes in.

😒 Just annoying

  • ✗Rename files in the computer folder.
  • ✗Write the caption for this week’s post.
  • ✗Organizing the inbox by color.
  • ✗Put together the pretty report nobody reads.

3

Find the bottleneck with numbers

Found two or three candidates? Now move beyond guesses. For each one, count three things during a typical week: time expense, money lost and errors that happened.

You don’t need a system. Put a piece of paper next to the computer and make a mark each time it happens. Add them up at the end of the week.

How to read the diagram: each bar’s length represents time spent. The lit bar is longer than the other three combined—and it also generates a penalty (bottom line). Marcos thought the problem was “posting more”; the numbers show that it’s the smallest bar.

4

Avoid automating what doesn’t hurt

The most common mistake isn’t technical. It’s choosing the wrong process. We tend to automate what is fun of automating or what appears — post, video, “replying bot” — and leave out what it costs, because it’s boring and nobody sees it.

And each connected agent is one more door to control (tracks 3 and 4). It’s not worth opening a door for a small gain.

How to read the diagram: The higher it is, the more the problem costs; the farther to the right, the more often it occurs. The lit-up box (high and to the left) is where money quietly leaks away. The red box is where almost everyone starts—and therefore gets disappointed.

✓ Good signs for automation

  • ✓It happens every week, the same way.
  • ✓When it fails, you can calculate the loss.
  • ✓You know how to say what "done right" means.

✗ Bad signs

  • ✗“Everyone’s doing it.”
  • ✗It happens once a month and takes five minutes.
  • ✗Every case is different and depends on your judgment.

⚠️ Attention

Bringing more customers into a process that’s already leaking doesn’t solve the problem: it makes the leak bigger. If the bottleneck is in the middle, flooding the entry point only makes the line longer—and the customer you lose now is one you paid to attract.

5

Run the 10× interview with an AI chat

It’s easy to dodge the question on your own. An AI chat helps you stay focused: it asks, you answer. One question at a time keep the conversation short and go straight to the bottleneck.

Here, the chat is just a assistant: it doesn’t connect to anything or change anything. You know the business, and you’re the one who answers.

🎯 Goal: leave with a candidate process and the number that proves what it costs

Paste into the AI chat you already use (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini). Replace what's inside < >.

Me entreviste sobre o meu negócio: <descreva em uma linha, ex.: salão de beleza com 3 cadeiras e 1 recepcionista>.

Comece por esta pergunta: se o meu negócio crescesse 10 vezes amanhã, que processo quebraria primeiro?

Regras da entrevista:
- Faça UMA pergunta por vez e espere a minha resposta.
- No máximo 6 perguntas.
- Para cada processo que eu citar, pergunte: quantas vezes por semana acontece, quanto tempo leva e o que acontece quando dá errado.
- Separe o que QUEBRA (perde cliente, dinheiro ou prazo) do que só INCOMODA.
- Não invente números: use só o que eu disser. Se eu não souber, escreva "estimar".

No fim, escreva:
1. o processo que quebra primeiro, em uma frase;
2. os números que eu dei (vezes por semana, tempo, prejuízo);
3. o que eu deveria contar na próxima semana para confirmar;
4. um processo que parece urgente mas só incomoda.
How to verify: the final answer has all 4 items, every number came from you (nothing you didn’t say), and item 1 is a process—not a wish like "sell more".

How Marcos's interview began

Marcos: Interview me about my business: an accounting firm with two people and around eighty clients…
Chat: If you had ten times as many customers tomorrow, what daily task would you be unable to keep up with?
Marcos: Review the documents clients send over WhatsApp. This already takes me all morning.
Chat: How many documents arrive each week, and what happens when one is missing?

💡 Practical tip

The conversation stays between you and the chat. Don’t paste customer names or documents: talk about the process, not people's. That's already the course's first security habit.

6

Choose one process, just one

The temptation is to solve everything at once. Don’t. One agent at a time, in one process at a time, is the only way to know whether it worked and stayed under control.

The other candidates don’t disappear: they go on a waitlist. Once the first one is running and measured, you can come back to them.

1

List the candidates

What came out of the 10× interview that breaks—not what bothers you.

2

Put the number beside it

Hours per week or reais per month. Without a number, the candidate waits until next week.

3

Start by cutting anything that affects money

Automatic payments and billing can wait until you’re in control (track 3). The first agent should make cheap mistakes.

4

Write the decision statement

“I’ll start with <process>, because today it costs <number> per <week/month>." Marcos wrote: "I’ll start with checking documents, because today it costs 10 hours a week and three fines a month."

Quick test (optional): Renata has three candidates. Which one should she choose first?
